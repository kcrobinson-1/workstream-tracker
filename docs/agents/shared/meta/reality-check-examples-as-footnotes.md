# Reality-Check Examples as Footnotes

**Shallow meta-rule.** Some rules are *forensic*: they exist
because a specific past incident burned a team. The incident
gives the rule its punch in the team's collective memory but
also pegs the rule to one tooling stack, one framework, one
specific past mistake. The rule below codifies the pattern of
keeping the broader principle in the rule body and demoting
the concrete incident to an illustrative footnote that
consumers can replace.

## The rule

When a rule's body is a *broader principle* and its motivating
example is a *specific past incident*:

- The **rule body** carries the broader principle, generic
  enough to apply across repos.
- The **specific incident** lives as an illustrative footnote
  attached to the rule, clearly marked as illustrative and
  replaceable.
- Consumers of the shared library are expected to replace the
  footnote with their own war story when they have one — the
  footnote earns its keep by making the rule memorable; the
  rule earns its keep without depending on the footnote.

## Why this pattern matters

Forensic rules without the demotion drift toward two failure
modes:

1. **Over-specific rule prose.** A rule body that names the
   specific tool, the specific framework, or the specific
   bug stops applying when the team changes tools. The
   principle stays valid; the prose has aged out of relevance.
2. **Lost punch.** A rule body that drops the incident
   entirely loses what made the rule memorable. Readers
   shrug at the abstract principle and re-trip the same
   trap.

The footnote pattern keeps both: the rule body is durable; the
incident lives where it can be swapped for a more locally
relevant one.

## How to apply it

When writing a rule that exists because of a past incident:

1. Write the rule body as a broader principle.
2. Identify the concrete incident that motivates the rule.
3. Attach the incident as a Markdown footnote (`[^name]:`) at
   the point in the rule body where it provides force.
4. Open the footnote with a clear marker that this is
   illustrative and consumers can replace it.
5. If the consumer has not yet had their own war story for
   this rule, leave the shipped footnote in place. If and
   when the consumer's own incident lands, swap the footnote
   content.

## What this rule does not say

This rule does not say "remove all concrete examples." It
says concrete examples that pin the rule to a specific
incident live as footnotes consumers can replace, not as
inline rule body. Rules whose abstract form does not need a
forensic anchor (the pre-edit gate, the scope guardrails)
don't need footnotes at all.
